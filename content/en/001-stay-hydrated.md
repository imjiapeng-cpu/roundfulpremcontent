# Facts on Fluids: How to Stay Hydrated

Water is the most essential nutrient your body requires — and yet most people routinely under-consume it. Whether you're training, working at a desk, or simply moving through a warm day, maintaining proper fluid balance has a direct impact on your energy, mood, focus, and physical performance.

## Why hydration matters more than you think

Your body is roughly 60% water by weight. Every organ, cell, and system depends on it. Water carries nutrients to cells, regulates body temperature, cushions joints, and flushes waste products. When fluid levels drop, the effects ripple across nearly every physiological process.

Even mild dehydration — as little as 1–2% of body weight lost as fluid — has been shown to impair cognitive function, reduce endurance, and elevate perceived effort during physical activity. At 3–4% dehydration, strength and aerobic capacity drop noticeably. By the time you feel thirsty, you are already behind.

## How much do you actually need?

General guidelines from health authorities recommend:

- **Men**: approximately 3.7 litres (about 13 cups) of total fluid per day
- **Women**: approximately 2.7 litres (about 9 cups) of total fluid per day

These totals include all fluid sources — water, beverages, and the water naturally present in food, which accounts for roughly 20% of daily fluid intake for most people.

Your individual needs depend on several factors: activity level (exercise increases losses through sweat significantly), climate (hot or humid conditions accelerate fluid loss), body size, and health status (fever, illness, pregnancy, and breastfeeding all increase demands). As a practical baseline, aim for about 2 litres of water from beverages daily, and adjust upward based on conditions.

## Recognising dehydration

Dehydration doesn't always announce itself dramatically. Common signs include:

- Dark yellow or amber-coloured urine (pale yellow is the target)
- Dry mouth and lips
- Fatigue or low energy without an obvious cause
- Headache
- Difficulty concentrating
- Reduced urine frequency

A simple daily check: look at the colour of your first urine of the morning. If it's consistently dark, you are likely not drinking enough throughout the day.

## Practical hydration habits

**Start before you're thirsty.** The thirst mechanism is a lagging indicator — by the time you notice it, fluid balance has already shifted. Build a habit of drinking regularly rather than reactively.

**Carry a water bottle.** The single most reliable predictor of whether someone meets their daily fluid needs is whether they have water within reach. Keep a bottle on your desk, in your bag, and in your car.

**Drink water with every meal.** Using meals as hydration anchors ensures at minimum three consistent intake points throughout the day.

**Adjust for exercise.** Before physical activity, drink 400–600 ml about two hours ahead of time. During sustained exercise, aim for 150–250 ml every 15–20 minutes. Afterwards, replace lost fluid by drinking roughly 1.5 times the volume you estimate losing through sweat.

**Account for heat.** On hot or humid days, increase intake proactively — even if you aren't exercising. Air-conditioned environments can also be subtly dehydrating due to low ambient humidity.

**Eat water-rich foods.** Fruits and vegetables contribute meaningfully to daily fluid intake. Cucumbers, watermelon, oranges, tomatoes, and leafy greens are all excellent sources.

**Be mindful of alcohol and caffeine.** Both act as diuretics, increasing fluid loss. They can be part of a balanced approach, but should not substitute for water when aiming to stay well hydrated.

## A note on sports drinks

For moderate daily activity and exercise lasting under an hour, plain water is sufficient. Sports drinks containing electrolytes become relevant for sustained high-intensity exercise (60 or more minutes) or activities involving significant sweating, where sodium and potassium replacement matters alongside fluid. Many commercially available sports drinks also carry substantial sugar — worth checking the label before reaching for them routinely.

## The bottom line

Staying well hydrated doesn't require any special products or complex routines. It comes down to consistent, small habits: keep water accessible, drink before you're thirsty, check the colour of your urine, and adjust when you're more active or the weather is warmer. These habits compound into meaningfully better energy, cognition, and physical performance over time.
